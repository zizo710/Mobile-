# Single-Name Convexity Map: Who Moves Most in Each Scenario

*Companion to [`us-market-2026-vs-1998-2002.md`](us-market-2026-vs-1998-2002.md).
Written 30 July 2026. Educational analysis of scenario sensitivity — not
investment advice, and option economics cited are structural, not live quotes.*

---

## Purpose

The base analysis handicapped four scenarios over ~2–3 years: full 1998–2002
repeat (~10–15%), serious AI-centric bear (~30–35%), corrections-but-no-bust
(~35–40%), clean escape (~15–20%), with ~50%+ odds of a melt-up leg first.
Index options mute those scenarios; the 1998–2002 episode did its violence in
single names (Lucent −99%, JDS Uniphase −98%, Cisco −86% — versus S&P −49%).
This document maps the 2026 cast onto the 1999–2000 archetypes and identifies
which names are *structurally* prone to the largest moves in each scenario.

---

## 1. The cast, by 1999–2000 archetype

### The stalled general — Nvidia (NVDA)
Cisco's role in the analog, with one huge difference: price. NVDA is up only
~3–9% YTD in 2026 despite data-centre revenue +92% y/y — the market has
rotated to the second-derivative trades and left the general behind at ~30x
forward (Cisco topped at ~130x). ~$5T cap, ~7% of the S&P.
**Convexity profile:** the best *upside* candidate in a blow-off. The Q1 2000
pattern was capital crowding into the biggest, most liquid theme names as the
tail weakened — a final leg likely runs through NVDA precisely because it has
lagged. Downside in the bust case is real but structurally smaller than the
rest of the complex (the multiple isn't the fragile part; the customer-capex
cliff is — the Cisco lesson, where real earnings still produced −86%).

### The momentum challenger — AMD
+150–170% YTD on the OpenAI 6GW MI450 deal and hyperscaler diversification
away from Nvidia. **Convexity profile:** high both ways — it's the momentum
expression of the melt-up, but a capex guide-down hits the challenger's
"revenue visibility" narrative harder than the incumbent's installed base.
Already up 2.5x this year, so the easy convexity has been harvested.

### The parabolic second derivative — SanDisk (SNDK), Micron (MU)
The JDS Uniphase / PMC-Sierra class of this cycle. SNDK ~+570% YTD (~$1,590),
MU ~+230–300% YTD (~$921), on genuinely spectacular fundamentals (MU revenue
+346% y/y; SNDK datacenter revenue +645%) and sell-side projections of DRAM
shortage "through 2030" — the 2026 rendition of 1999's "internet traffic
doubles every 100 days." Memory is a commodity cycle being priced as a
secular monopoly. These names fell 10–15% in a single week when capex
sentiment cracked in late July, then V-bounced.
**Convexity profile:** the largest two-way movers in the entire complex. In a
melt-up they are the blow-off's spear tip; in any bear scenario, memory's
history (repeated −60–80% cycle downturns, no exceptions in four decades)
plus a +570% YTD entry point implies the biggest percentage unwinds among
real businesses. The JDSU precedent cuts both ways: it fell 98% *after* first
doubling twice more — shorting parabolas early is how bears die.

### The debt-financed pure-plays — CoreWeave (CRWV), Nebius (NBIS), IREN
The CLEC class — the direct heirs of Winstar and Global Crossing. CoreWeave:
~$17.3B debt against a $2.27B cash balance, quarterly capex ($7.7B) running
at nearly 4x quarterly revenue ($2.08B, +112% y/y), $20B+ raised in 2026
alone including GPU-collateralised facilities, loan terms sweetened to get
deals done — and as of 30 July, **CDS markets pricing ~50% five-year default
odds**. Nebius fell 15% in a week on funding concerns; the group tumbled when
Meta signalled it would build cloud capacity itself (hyperscalers competing
with their own suppliers — exactly what the carriers did to the CLECs), then
squeezed ~30% in a day when Citadel reportedly bought Situational Awareness's
public equity book.
**Convexity profile:** the highest gross convexity in the market — genuine
zero-or-multiple outcomes — but the least *mispriced*: with CDS at 50%,
option markets already charge bust-level premia, and 30%-in-a-day squeezes
make short structures lethal to carry. The interesting fact is that the
credit market is already voting; equity hasn't fully followed.

### The Lucent-shaped megacap — Oracle (ORCL)
The single clearest credit-transmission candidate among large caps, and the
most analytically interesting name on the board. The bull story is a $638B
backlog; the balance sheet says: net debt $97.6B (+20% y/y), FY26 free cash
flow of **−$23.7B** (from −$0.4B a year earlier), ~$95B gross capex planned
for FY27 requiring ~$40B of new external capital, Moody's on negative outlook
warning of 4x leverage. Roughly **half the backlog (~$300B) is one customer**
— OpenAI via Stargate — under a contract that doesn't even begin until 2027,
signed by a counterparty that itself needs continuous external funding.
Crucially: the stock is already at a ~14–18 month low *while the index made
all-time highs* — the market is quietly re-rating it from software multiple
toward levered-utility multiple.
**Convexity profile:** the best *risk-adjusted* bear expression in the
complex. Megacap option liquidity, materially lower implied vol than the
speculative names, an idiosyncratic catalyst path (it must raise ~$40B/yr in
public markets — every debt deal is a referendum) that can play out even in
the no-bust scenario, and the direct 2000–02 template: Lucent, whose real
revenues and giant order book were revealed as vendor-financed concentration
risk. Offset: if the melt-up broadens or Stargate revenue lands early, a
crowded bear unwinds hard.

### The multiple with no floor — Palantir (PLTR)
Trailing P/E ~150x, forward ~84x, **~41x sales** at ~$124 — against genuinely
excellent fundamentals (revenue +85% y/y, guidance ~+71%). This is the 2000
Microsoft / 2021 PLTR setup: the business does not need to disappoint for the
stock to halve; only the multiple regime needs to change. Its own 2022
history (−85% peak-to-trough while revenue *grew*) is the proof of concept.
**Convexity profile:** the purest multiple-compression bet — pays in every
bear scenario regardless of AI fundamentals, and doesn't require a credit
event. Downside as a bear expression: highest retail cult ownership in the
market; in a melt-up it can double from any level (it has before).

### The integrators — Dell (DELL), Super Micro (SMCI)
The Compaq/Gateway class: enormous AI order books ($51.3B and $60B backlogs)
on thin integration margins, whipsawing violently on each other's updates
(DELL −14% one week, +9% the next on an SMCI read-through). **Convexity
profile:** high realised vol but low analytical edge — they are derivative of
hyperscaler capex with no pricing power either way. The first hyperscaler
guide-down hits their backlogs first and hardest, but the same trade is
cleaner in ORCL or the neoclouds. Pass.

### The canary corner — Oklo (OKLO) and speculative nuclear
OKLO −42% YTD, −28% in a month, *while the index made highs* — the
pre-revenue concept tail is already in its own private bear market, even with
government programs ($200M award alongside X-Energy) and Microsoft/Nvidia
partnerships attached. Meanwhile the profitable power names (Vistra +5%,
Constellation ~flat) are fine.
**Signal value more than trade value:** in the 1998–2002 template, the
speculative tail dying while the generals hold is the Act 3→4 transition
signature. This is arguably the single most bearish *breadth* fact in the
current tape — and it's already partially spent as a trade.

## 2. Scenario × name sensitivity matrix

Directional sensitivity of each name per scenario (qualitative, peak-move
potential over the scenario's life, not a price target):

| Name | Melt-up first (~50% path odds) | No-bust chop (35–40%) | Serious bear (30–35%) | Full bust (10–15%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NVDA | **+40–80%** (crowding into the general) | flat | −40–60% | −70% (Cisco path) |
| AMD | +50–100% | −20% | −50–70% | −80% |
| MU / SNDK | **+50–150%** (blow-off spear tip) | −30% (cycle wobble) | **−50–70%** | **−80%+** (memory cycle + entry point) |
| CRWV / neoclouds | +50–100% (squeeze-prone) | ±50% (binary refis) | −70–90% | **−100%** (Winstar outcome; CDS already at 50%) |
| ORCL | +20–30% | **−20–40% (works even here — own funding math)** | −50–60% | −70–80% (Lucent path, survivorship likely) |
| PLTR | +50–100% (cult melt-up) | −30–50% (multiple regime) | **−60–80%** | −85% (its own 2022 replay) |
| DELL / SMCI | +30–60% | ±30% whipsaw | −50–70% | −80% |
| OKLO | +100% (squeezes) | −50% | −80% | −95% |

Two structural readings from the matrix:

1. **ORCL is the only name whose bear case doesn't require a bear market.**
   Its funding calendar is its own catalyst. That makes it the highest
   probability-weighted bear expression, though not the highest payoff.
2. **Memory is the only cohort with top-quartile convexity in *both*
   directions** — which is exactly what the JDSU archetype looked like in
   late 1999. It is the defining single-name trade of this phase, and the
   most dangerous to be early on.

## 3. A $5k single-name options book (hypothetical, educational)

### The contract-size reality check first

At ~$921 (MU) and ~$1,590 (SNDK), one option contract controls $92k–$159k of
notional; even a 12-month put *spread* on these can cost more per contract
than an entire sleeve of a $5k book. Practical consequences: the memory leg
may need to be expressed via the semiconductor ETF (SMH) or via whichever
memory name's contract economics actually fit, and the book must concentrate
in 3–4 structures rather than diversify. Single-name IV on this complex is
also brutal — CRWV/PLTR/SNDK options carry crash-level implied vol already —
so spreads (selling the overpriced far wing) matter even more than in the
index book. All legs 12+ months where available; expiries bracketing the
September FOMC and autumn capex guidance.

### The book

| Sleeve | Size | Structure | Archetype being traded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core bear | ~$1,500 (30%) | ORCL long-dated put spreads (long ~10–15% OTM, short ~40% OTM), 12–18 months | Lucent: credit transmission at megacap liquidity and sane IV; works in 3 of 4 scenarios |
| Cycle bear | ~$1,000 (20%) | Memory put spreads, 12–18 months, **entered in halves** — half now, half only after the parabola breaks trend (first lower high) | JDSU: biggest % unwind among real businesses; tranching is the concession to blow-off risk |
| Zero ticket | ~$400 (8%) | CRWV far-OTM long-dated puts, sized to burn | Winstar: the −100% outcome; accept that CDS at 50% means you overpay, and that total loss of the sleeve is the likeliest result |
| Multiple bear | ~$600 (12%) | PLTR put spread, 12+ months | 41x sales: pays on regime change alone, no credit event needed |
| Melt-up leg | ~$1,000 (20%) | NVDA call spreads, 6–9 months, ~10% OTM long strike | The stalled general: cheapest large-cap expression of the Act-3 final leg |
| Dry powder | ~$500 (10%) | Unspent until a signal fires (below) | The 2000 playbook: tops announce themselves |

### Why this shape

- Versus the index book, the bear side migrates from QQQ to **ORCL + memory +
  PLTR + CRWV** — four *different* failure mechanisms (funding calendar,
  commodity cycle, multiple regime, credit event). That is deliberate: they
  pay on different catalysts and dates, where five QQQ puts pay on one.
- The melt-up leg stays in a single general rather than the challenger (AMD
  +150% already) or the parabolas (spreads too expensive at their IV). If
  the blow-off comes, history says it comes to the biggest name lagging.
- OKLO, DELL, SMCI are analytically interesting and deliberately excluded:
  the canary is already dead (−42%), and the integrators offer vol without
  edge.

### Signal-triggered adjustments (the powder's job list)

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Index bounce fails at a lower high | Deploy powder into the ORCL and memory bear sleeves; the Act-4 clock starts |
| A hyperscaler cuts 2027 capex guidance | Add to memory/integrator-adjacent bear exposure immediately — this was the March 2000 telecom-order moment, and it will gap |
| CRWV completes a large refinancing on decent terms | Kill the zero ticket, don't average it — the Winstar trade needs the funding window to *shut* |
| Fed hikes in September and the market takes it badly | Roll the NVDA call spread down in size; the 1999 template's melt-up window narrows once hikes bite |
| Memory names make new highs while NVDA still lags | Patience on the cycle-bear second half; the parabola isn't done. Do not fight the spear tip |
| OKLO-style weakness spreads to profitable names (VST, CEG) | Breadth deterioration climbing the quality curve — add bear, trim the melt-up leg |

## 4. Honest expectations and single-name-specific risks

- **The modal outcome is still losing most of the $5k.** In the 35–40%
  chop scenario every sleeve bleeds; single names add idiosyncratic ways to
  be right on thesis and lose anyway (ORCL rallies on one good debt deal;
  PLTR squeezes on an earnings beat).
- **Squeeze risk is not theoretical.** The neocloud complex moved ~+30% in a
  single session this week on one fund's portfolio changing hands. Bear
  structures must be defined-risk (spreads, small tickets) — no naked short
  legs anywhere in this complex.
- **Earnings binaries:** every name here has 2–4 scheduled events inside the
  option's life that can gap 15–30%. Long-dated structures amortise this;
  short-dated ones are coin flips on IV crush.
- **Correlation caveat:** in the bust scenario all four bear sleeves pay at
  once — which means in expectation they are partially the *same* trade, and
  the book is smaller than it looks. The diversification is across catalysts
  and timing, not across the terminal scenario.
- **The market has partially caught up.** CRWV CDS at 50%, ORCL at 18-month
  lows, OKLO −42%: the 2026 tape is already differentiating in a way the
  1999 tape did not until much later. The cheap convexity is in the names
  where equity hasn't yet followed credit (ORCL), not the ones where it has.
