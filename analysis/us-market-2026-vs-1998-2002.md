# Where We Stand: The 2024–26 AI Cycle vs. the 1998–2002 Boom and Bust

*Written 30 July 2026. Market data as of the July 28–30 sessions. Not investment
advice — this is a framework for thinking about the analog, not a forecast.*

---

## TL;DR

Mapped against the 1998–2002 arc, the current market most closely resembles
**mid-to-late 1999**: the post-crisis easing that fuelled the run-up is behind
us, the Fed has pivoted hawkish (three FOMC dissents *for a hike* this week,
September live), oil is spiking on a Middle East conflict, breadth has
narrowed to record concentration, the capex boom is now visibly outrunning
cash flow, and vendor-financing loops have re-appeared at the centre of the
trade. The first serious capex-skepticism corrections have hit (March 2026,
and again this week — Nasdaq 100 briefly into correction on 28 July), but each
has so far been bought, which is *also* what 1999 looked like.

The analog argues we are **late-cycle but not obviously at the top**: in the
1999 template, the final leg (October 1999 – March 2000, Nasdaq roughly +80%)
came *after* conditions looked this stretched. The three biggest differences
from 1999 — megacap profitability, real revenues, and a far more restrained
IPO market — argue the eventual unwind, whenever it comes, starts from a
sturdier foundation than March 2000. The biggest *rhyme* that should worry
bulls: the financing of the boom has quietly shifted from internal cash to
debt and circular vendor structures, which is precisely the transition that
turned the telecom build-out from a boom into a bust.

## 2. The 1998–2002 arc, briefly

The dot-com episode wasn't a single event; it was a five-act structure. The
whole point of the comparison is figuring out which act we're in.

**Act 1 — Crisis and rescue (Aug–Nov 1998).** Russia defaults, LTCM implodes,
the S&P drops ~19%. The Fed cuts three times (5.50% → 4.75%) into a strong
economy. The rescue works too well: liquidity floods into the narrowest,
fastest-growing part of the market.

**Act 2 — The melt-up (1999).** Nasdaq +85.6% in a single year — still the
largest annual gain ever for a major US index. Y2K remediation and internet
build-out spending give the boom a "real capex" justification. 486 IPOs, with
average first-day pops near 70%. Breadth deteriorates all year: by late 1999
the average stock is flat-to-down while the index makes highs. The Fed starts
hiking in June 1999 — and the market accelerates *upward* for nine more months.

**Act 3 — Blow-off and top (Oct 1999 – Mar 2000).** The most violent part of
the entire advance: Nasdaq roughly doubles from the October 1999 low to
5,048.62 on 10 March 2000. Cisco briefly becomes the world's most valuable
company at ~$550B, ~130x forward earnings. CAPE peaks ~44 (Dec 1999). Oil has
tripled off its 1998 low ($11 → $34); the Fed keeps hiking to 6.5% (May 2000).

**Act 4 — The break (Mar 2000 – Mar 2001).** No single catalyst — the
marginal buyer simply ran out as the Fed drained liquidity and Y2K-inflated
capex went ex-growth. Rallies fail at lower highs (the giveaway that
distinguished the top from every prior dip). Dot-coms with no revenue die
first; then the *profitable* infrastructure suppliers — Cisco, Lucent, Nortel
— discover their order books were bubble demand, partly vendor-financed by
themselves. Lucent's loans to CLECs come back as write-downs.

**Act 5 — The long unwind (2001–Oct 2002).** Recession (Mar–Nov 2001), 9/11,
Enron, WorldCom. Telecoms had laid vastly more fibre than demand could absorb
— only a low-single-digit share was lit by 2002 — financed with debt that
produced the sector's ~$1T wipeout and a bankruptcy wave. Final damage: Nasdaq
−78% (5,048 → 1,114, Oct 2002), S&P 500 −49% (1,527 → 777). Nasdaq did not
reclaim its 2000 high until 2015.

## 3. The phase mapping

| 1998–2002 | 2024–2026 | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| LTCM/Russia crisis → 3 Fed cuts into a strong economy (late 1998) | 2024–25 easing cycle down to 3.50–3.75% into a resilient economy | **Strong.** Same shape: insurance easing that landed on a narrow, story-driven leadership group |
| Y2K + internet/telecom capex as the "real economy" justification | AI data-centre capex: $660–690B in 2026 from five companies, nearly 2x y/y | **Strong, with a twist.** Y2K spending had a hard deadline and a known end; AI capex is open-ended — both bigger and less obviously self-terminating |
| 1999 melt-up; Fed starts hiking June 1999 but market keeps running | 2025 melt-up; strongest Nasdaq quarter since 2020 in Q2 2026; Fed turns hawkish July 2026 with September hike live | **Strong.** The policy inflection has just happened. In the template, the top came ~9 months *after* the first hike |
| Oil triples 1998→2000; CPI accelerates; 10yr rises toward 6.5% | Oil spiking on Iran hostilities; CPI 3.5–4.2%; 10yr at 2026 highs (~4.66%) | **Moderate-strong.** Same direction, milder magnitude |
| Breadth narrows through 1999; top-10 weight reaches ~25% at peak | Top-10 weight 41–43% — far *beyond* the 2000 extreme; Nvidia alone ~7% vs. Cisco's ~4.5% peak | **Exceeded.** Concentration is the one dimension where 2026 is already past March-2000 levels |
| Generals stall, soldiers rally: leadership rotates into secondary plays late in the move | Nvidia +9% YTD while memory/infrastructure names lead; Dell −13%, memory names −10–15% in one week when sentiment cracked | **Strong — and late-cycle.** Rotation down the quality curve inside the theme is a classic Act-3 signature |
| Vendor financing: Lucent/Nortel lend to CLECs to buy their own equipment | Nvidia equity into OpenAI tied to purchases of Nvidia systems ($100B, potentially ~$250B); neocloud debt secured on GPUs | **Strong in form, different in degree.** 1999's borrowers had no revenue; today's are loss-making but revenue-generating, and hyperscaler customers are wildly profitable |
| Capex starts consuming the industry's cash (telecom FCF deeply negative by 1999–2000, debt fills the gap) | Hyperscaler debt share of capex 9% → 32% in ~18 months; Alphabet's first-ever negative-FCF quarter; ~$1.5T debt wave projected | **Strong — and the most important rhyme.** This is the transition that converted the telecom boom into a bust |
| Jan 2000 wobble bought; final highs March 2000; then rallies start failing | March 2026 correction bought hard (record Q2); late-July 2026 correction being bought as we write | **Undetermined.** This is exactly the juncture where 1999 (dip = buy) and 2000 (dip = distribution) diverge |
| 1999 IPO mania: 486 deals, ~70% average first-day pops, profitless concepts | 2026 IPOs: +164% y/y in proceeds but institutional-led, selective, valuation-sensitive | **Weak fit — bullish divergence.** The public-market on-ramp for junk equity that defined 1999 largely isn't here; the speculative financing is happening in private credit/equity instead |

**Where that puts us:** roughly **August–December 1999** on the template —
after the hawkish turn, into the oil shock, with concentration and valuation
extremes in place and the first hard corrections behind us, but before the
blow-off/failure sequence that defines a top. The two live questions from the
template are (a) whether this week's bounce extends into an Act-3-style final
leg, and (b) whether rallies start failing at lower highs — which in 2000 was
the first unambiguous behavioural break.

## 4. The scorecard: 2026 vs. the 2000 extreme

| Dimension | March 2000 | July 2026 | 2026 vs. 2000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shiller CAPE | ~44 | ~41 | ~93% of the way there |
| Index forward P/E | S&P ~25x; Nasdaq >100x | S&P ~21x; tech ~36x | Well short of 2000's insanity |
| Leader's valuation | Cisco ~130x forward | Nvidia ~30x forward (Mag 7 avg ~26x) | Dramatically cheaper vs. earnings |
| Leader's profitability | Cisco profitable; most of the theme was not | The theme's core is the most profitable cohort in corporate history | Fundamental divergence |
| Top-10 concentration | ~25% | 41–43% | **Far beyond 2000** |
| Largest single weight | ~4.5% (Cisco/MSFT era) | ~7% (Nvidia) | **Beyond 2000** |
| Policy direction | Hiked 4.75% → 6.50% | Cuts ended; hawkish hold, hike risk | Same direction, earlier innings, lower level |
| Real 10yr yield backdrop | ~6.5% nominal | ~4.66% nominal | Less restrictive |
| Oil / inflation impulse | Oil 3x off lows; CPI accelerating | Oil spiking on Iran; CPI 3.5–4.2% | Same shape, milder |
| Capex funding | Telecom: heavily debt-funded | Hyperscalers: 32% debt-funded and rising fast | Converging on the danger zone |
| Vendor financing | Lucent/Nortel → revenue-less CLECs | Nvidia → OpenAI/neoclouds (loss-making but real revenue) | Present, better collateralised |
| IPO froth | Extreme (record deals, ~70% pops) | Reviving, disciplined | Nowhere near |
| Retail/derivative froth | Extreme (day-trading era) | Elevated but not 1999-grade in public markets | Below 2000 |

**Reading the scorecard:** valuation is ~90% of the 2000 extreme,
concentration is *past* it, policy and macro are directionally aligned but
milder, and the equity-froth channels (IPOs, profitless concepts) are far
tamer. The stress has instead migrated to the **credit and private side** —
which is where the 1998–2002 episode did its real economic damage (telecom
debt), not the equity side that gets remembered.

## 5. What's genuinely different this time

1. **The leaders earn their valuations — for now.** In 2000, the theme in
   aggregate destroyed capital. In 2026, Mag 7 trade ~26x forward with 30%+
   expected earnings growth. A 2000-style −78% in the leadership would require
   the *earnings* to be bubble artifacts, not just the multiples. That said,
   this was exactly the Cisco defence in 1999 — Cisco's earnings were real
   until its customers' capex was revealed to be debt-financed bubble demand.
   The equivalent question today: how much of Nvidia's revenue is funded by
   debt raised against optimistic AI returns — including debt Nvidia itself
   backstops?

2. **Depreciation is the new fibre glut.** Telecom overbuilt 25-year-life
   fibre; AI overbuilds 3–5-year-life GPUs. The overcapacity risk arrives
   *faster* but also self-corrects faster — dark fibre stayed dark for a
   decade, dark GPUs would be obsolete in four years. The bear translation:
   the depreciation wall hits earnings in 2027–28 even in the no-crash case.

3. **The financing shift is the real 1999 rhyme.** 9% → 32% debt-funded capex
   in eighteen months, a projected $1.5T issuance wave, negative FCF at
   Alphabet, GPU-collateralised neocloud debt, and circular equity-for-orders
   structures. Every element has a direct 1999 telecom antecedent. This — not
   the CAPE — is the variable to watch, because credit is what turns a
   drawdown into a bust.

4. **No 1999-style public-equity on-ramp for junk.** The marginal speculative
   dollar in 1999 flowed through IPOs into public markets, where its
   destruction was visible and reflexive. Today it flows through private
   credit and mega-cap balance sheets. The excess is likely comparable; its
   unwind mechanics (slower marks, less daily reflexivity, more concentrated
   holders) are different — arguably less crash-prone and more grind-prone.

5. **Concentration cuts both ways.** Top-10 at 43% means index investors are
   making a 10-stock bet that they weren't even in 2000. But it also means
   the index's fate rests on the ten most profitable, most liquid balance
   sheets on earth rather than 1999's long tail of cash-burners.

## 6. Signposts: what would say "March 2000" has arrived

From the 1998–2002 playbook, the top wasn't marked by valuation (extreme for
years) but by *behavioural and fundamental breaks*. The 2026 equivalents:

- **Rallies failing at lower highs** in the leadership complex — the single
  cleanest 2000 signal. This week's bounce is therefore informative either way.
- **The first hyperscaler capex guide-down.** The 2000 top in telecom equipment
  came when carriers cut orders, not when valuations peaked. Watch 2027 capex
  guidance this earnings season; Alphabet's negative FCF print is the first
  crack in the "we can fund this forever" narrative.
- **Credit spreads on AI/neocloud debt widening** while equities hold — credit
  led equity in 2000–01. The $1.5T issuance wave gives this cycle a real
  high-yield/private-credit tape to watch, which 1999's equity mania lacked.
- **A vendor-financing write-down** — any Nvidia disclosure resembling
  Lucent's 2000 CLEC reserves, or a neocloud restructuring that marks GPU
  collateral down.
- **The Fed actually hiking** into the oil shock. June 1999's first hike
  preceded the top by nine months; May 2000's 50bp came two months after it.
  A September 2026 hike would start that clock.
- **Rotation into "safety within the theme"** — in Q1 2000 money crowded into
  the biggest names as the tail died. A blow-off in Mag 7 while equal-weight
  and the memory/infrastructure complex roll over would be late-Act-3
  behaviour.

Conversely, the signposts that the analog *breaks bullishly*: capex converts
into visible AI revenue growth at the application layer (the thing 1999-era
telecom never got), the debt share of capex stabilises, inflation resumes
falling and takes the September hike off the table.

## 7. Downside arithmetic, if the analog completes

Purely mechanical translation, not a prediction:

- A **2000–02 S&P-style unwind (−49%)** from ~7,400 → ~3,800, i.e. round-trip
  to early-2024 levels.
- A **Nasdaq-style −78%** has no credible 2026 path without the Mag 7's
  earnings being revealed as circular — the profitability difference is real.
  The closer template for the leaders is **Cisco**: real company, real
  earnings, −86% anyway, because 130x-forward met a customer-capex cliff. At
  ~30x forward, Nvidia's equivalent multiple-compression risk is severe but
  categorically smaller.
- The historically likelier shape given profitable leaders + credit-financed
  capex is the **2000–02 telecom outcome transplanted to credit**: equity
  drawdown in the −25–40% range for the theme, with the deeper losses taken
  in AI-adjacent debt and private marks, and a multi-year earnings grind as
  the depreciation wall and capex normalisation compress margins.
- And the flip side, from the same template: exiting in August 1999 meant
  missing an ~80% final leg. Late-cycle is not end-of-cycle; the 1999 lesson
  cuts against both complacency *and* premature capitulation.

---

## 8. Bottom line

On the 1998–2002 clock, late July 2026 reads as **roughly Q3–Q4 1999**: the
easing that built the boom has given way to a tightening bias, an oil shock is
in progress, concentration has exceeded the 2000 extreme, the capex engine is
now debt-financed at the margin, vendor-financing loops are back, and the
market has begun to correct on capex skepticism — twice — and been bought both
times. The bubble-defining *equity* excesses of 1999 (profitless IPOs, triple-
digit index multiples) are largely absent; the bust-defining *credit* dynamics
of 2000–02 (debt-funded capex, circular financing, cash-flow deterioration at
the buyers) are increasingly present.

The 1999 template says the melt-up can extend well past this point, and that
the top will announce itself not through valuation but through failed rallies,
a capex guide-down, and credit leading equity lower. Those are the three dials
to watch through the autumn — starting with whether this week's bounce makes a
new high or the first lower high of the cycle.
